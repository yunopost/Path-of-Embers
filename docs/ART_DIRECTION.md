# Path of Embers — current art direction

Updated by Aaron's direction, 11 September 2026. This supersedes older golden-hour, warm-brown linework, warm-equals-alive and fixed 70/30 palette requirements. Existing work may be rebuilt when it measurably improves playability or aesthetics; retain useful assets and avoid churn.

## Palette and lighting

The Witch is the preferred reference for color balance: deep teal, cool greens, pale neutral hair, slate and charcoal, with natural skin and small intentional warm details. Use varied cool and neutral hues across the world: slate blue-grey, desaturated teal, moss green, muted violet and stone. Avoid an overall sepia, amber, bronze or golden-hour wash. Warmth belongs to local fire, small material details, or deliberate character accents; it must not tint every surface. A warm light source can occur when a scene calls for it, but sunset is not the default. Do not turn every character into a warm silhouette. Preserve their distinct palettes.

Keep painted gouache texture and expressive ink contours. Linework may be charcoal, slate or a local dark color; warm brown is not mandatory. Keep the world suspended in stillness, but do not use that story premise to impose a sunset color grade. Avoid glossy 3D rendering, oversaturation and bloom that obscures silhouettes.

## Combat composition and scale

Use Chaos Zero Nightmare's combat composition and relative actor scale as a reference, while retaining Path of Embers' own art and action clock. Compose the background around the actual HUD and hand, rather than laying characters over a finished landscape.

For a 16:9 combat image:

- Reserve the upper 18% for HUD and navigation. Put the distant horizon around 35% height.
- Provide a continuous, level, readable ground plane through the middle/lower battlefield. Party feet and ordinary enemy feet share a baseline around 68% height.
- Party centers occupy approximately x=18%–42%; enemies occupy approximately x=65%–86%. Keep large scenery out of their silhouette regions and target indicators.
- Ordinary humanoids should read at approximately 25%–30% of viewport height, adjusted for anatomy and perspective. Use body height, not PNG canvas dimensions or weapon width, to judge scale. Preserve aspect ratios. Bosses are visibly larger, within the available HUD/hand clearance.
- Reserve the bottom 22%–27% for resting cards. Raised cards may cover part of the battlefield temporarily, but the resting hand should not consume nearly half the screen.
- Avoid giant foreground wagon wheels, high-angle floor views, pillars, and decorative props in actor positions. Put tall scene detail behind the staging floor or at the far edges.

Judge backgrounds with party and enemy sprites composited in-game at 1280×720, 1600×900 and 1920×1080. Check foot alignment, actor contrast, enemy intentions and visible card headers. A beautiful standalone image is insufficient if it fails those checks.

## UI and card hand

Keep primary combat actions on the left, draw at left, discard at right and the hand centered. Put How to Play with navigation/help, outside the combat action rail. Resting cards may overlap; hover or selection must raise a complete readable card. Stable pointer hit regions, predictable stacking, cancellation and legal targeting matter more than decorative motion. Draw from the left, resolve the play, then exit toward the appropriate pile. Do not import CZN's end-turn or AP-reset rules.

## Asset acceptance

Character sprites must have real alpha transparency, tightly trimmed canvases, intact weapons and clean edge colors. Never accept a painted checkerboard as transparency. Review color balance, body scale and baseline together in the actual scene. Save replacement assets under new names until reviewed. Record generation prompts, deviations and verification. Local background removal is authorized for the generated character cutouts in this handoff; do not confuse it with permission to recolor unrelated assets silently.
